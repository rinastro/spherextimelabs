How it works

Three steps from coordinates to discovery

Enter

Pick a patch of sky

Type Right Ascension and declination, look up an object by name, or pick one of the test targets. Every view gets a shareable link.

Load

Fetch SPHEREx cutouts

Small cutouts come straight from NASA/IPAC’s Infrared Science Archive, then get rotated onto one north-up grid so frames line up.

Blink

Play, stack, subtract

Watch the frames as an animation, add images from the same visit to beat the noise, and subtract epochs to make movers pop out.

Why motion matters

Pluto was found by blinking two photographs.

Comparing images of the same field taken at different times is one of the oldest discovery tools in astronomy. Nearby stars and brown dwarfs drift against the background, asteroids and comets race across it, and variable stars change brightness.

SPHEREx maps the whole sky every six months in 102 near-infrared colours. That is far more data than anyone can inspect alone, so SkyFolder makes the inspection quick enough for anyone to help.

What’s in the folder

Tools built around SPHEREx data

Blink

Animated time series

Every image covering your position, sorted by date and played as a loop. Step frame by frame with the arrow keys.

Stack

Per-epoch co-adds

Combine all images from one visit, or one detector at a time, for a cleaner picture of each epoch.

Subtract

Difference imaging

Subtract the first epoch, the previous one, or the median sky. Static stars vanish; anything that moved leaves a dark–bright pair.

Spectrum

Spectra and light curves

Click any pixel to measure brightness across up to 102 wavelengths, or follow it over time to catch variable stars.

Overlay

Gaia DR3 and AllWISE

Gaia positions move with their proper motions frame by frame. A full Aladin Lite sky atlas sits below the viewer.

Share

Links and embeds

Coordinates and settings live in the URL. Copy a link, save a PNG, or embed the viewer on another site.
