Test targets

Five objects to calibrate your eye

These targets were picked to test the tool: three that move and two that change brightness. Each button opens the viewer with settings that suit the object.

Mover · nearby star

Barnard’s Star

Fastest proper motion of any star, about 10.3″ per year

A red dwarf only six light-years away. Between SPHEREx visits six months apart it shifts by about five arcseconds, close to one SPHEREx pixel. Over a year the step is easy to see when you blink epoch stacks.

17h57m47.58s

Mover · brown dwarfs

Epsilon Indi B

Two brown dwarfs orbiting the star Epsilon Indi A

The pair Ba and Bb sit less than an arcsecond apart, so SPHEREx sees them as a single point. The system moves almost 4.7″ per year together with its host star, about 1500″ away. Compare the long-wavelength detectors, where cold brown dwarfs are brightest.

22h04m23.07s

Mover · coldest brown dwarf

WISE 0855−0714

About 8″ per year, with a temperature near 250 K

This object is almost invisible at short wavelengths and brightest near 4.5 µm, so it opens on detector D6 with the median sky subtracted. The coordinates are those given in the challenge. The object has moved west since then, so look a little to the right of the marker.

About the positions. For the two fast movers with well-measured proper motions, SkyFolder moves the J2000 catalogue position forward to 2026.0 before searching, so the target lands near the centre of the field. Objects you look up by name get the same correction when the name service reports a proper motion.
