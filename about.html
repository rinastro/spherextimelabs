About

A blink comparator for everyone

SkyFolder is our answer to the “Planet X and SPHEREx” challenge of the 2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge: a public web tool that shows how the SPHEREx sky changes over time.

The challenge

Too much sky for one person

SPHEREx images more than a billion objects in 102 colours, twice a year. Comets, asteroids, nearby stars and brown dwarfs give themselves away by moving between images. The task: let anyone enter a position and watch the images play back like a short film.

Our approach

Simple first, deep when needed

One search box and one play button get you started. Stacking, subtraction, spectra, overlays and shareable links are there when you want them, without leaving the page or installing anything.

What we built against the brief

  • Enter RA and declination (or an object name) and see a time series of SPHEREx images played as an animation.
  • Tested on Barnard’s Star, Epsilon Indi B, WISE 0855−0714, RW Aurigae and R Draconis, each available in one click.
  • Easy to update: data releases live in a single configuration list and are queried live from IRSA.
  • Creative use of the 102 bands: per-detector selection, noise-weighted stacking of all six detectors, and spectra built on the fly.
  • Adding and subtracting images taken at different times: epoch stacks plus three kinds of difference imaging.
  • User-selectable field of view from 2′ to 30′.
  • Overlays from other missions: Gaia DR3 with proper motions applied per frame, and AllWISE, 2MASS, DSS and Pan-STARRS through Aladin Lite.
  • Query by URL and embed in other websites with one click.
  • Metadata display, including quality-flag maps and per-pixel flag bits.

Under the hood

The site is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step and no server. It can be hosted on GitHub Pages or any static host. All processing happens in the visitor’s browser: archive queries, FITS reading, WCS resampling with distortion correction, stacking, photometry and plotting.
